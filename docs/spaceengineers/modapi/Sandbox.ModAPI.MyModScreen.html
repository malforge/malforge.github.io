Sandbox.ModAPI

MyModScreen

Assembly: Sandbox.Game.dll

public abstract class MyModScreen

A GUI screen owned by a mod: a real entry in the game's screen stack, so it takes focus, starves gameplay of input while it is open, fades in and out with the game's transitions, and closes on Escape.

Derive from this, override the callbacks you need, and call Open() . The screen itself is empty: draw its content from MyModScreen.OnDraw(float)prohibited with MyScreenSpaceText and MyScreenSpaceSprite , left on the default BelowHud layer, which composites in place inside the screen. Multiply colours by the alpha passed to MyModScreen.OnDraw(float)prohibited so the content fades with the screen, and hit test with TryGetCursorPosition(out Vector2) , whose coordinates are the ones the content was drawn at. Anything that has to stay inside a panel - a scrolling list, a progress fill - is clipped with UsingScissorRectangle(RectangleF, MyScreenSpaceLayer) .

Because the screen carries no controls, it is a mouse and keyboard surface: a gamepad can close it but cannot point at anything, as pointing in this GUI is a matter of control focus.

Everything here must be called from the update thread, and a screen never outlives its session.

Methods

Member Description
Close() Closes the screen with the usual fade out. MyModScreen.OnClosed(bool)prohibited arrives once the fade has finished.
CloseNow() Closes the screen without a fade, delivering MyModScreen.OnClosed(bool)prohibited before returning.
Hide() Transitions the screen out but keeps it in the stack, so Unhide() brings it back with its state intact.
Open() Puts the screen on top of the screen stack, where it takes focus and input. Does nothing when the screen is already open, and logs and returns rather than throwing when a screen cannot be opened at all: on a dedicated server, off the update thread, with no running session, or once too many mod screens are open.
